Why Were Preference Optimization Algorithms Like DPO and ORPO Introduced, and What Limitations

of Standard SFT Do They Address?

In normal SFT, the model is trained only on the correct or chosen response.

Example:

Prompt:

How should | treat a mild fever?

Target Response:

Rest, stay hydrated, monitor your temperature,

and contact a doctor if symptoms become severe.

The SFT objective increases the probability of the target response.

However, SFT does not explicitly tell the model:

This response is preferred.

This other response is rejected.

The ORPO researchers observed that while SFT increases the probability of chosen responses, it may also

increase the probability of rejected responses because both responses can contain similar language,

tokens, topics and writing patterns.

SFT has no direct mechanism for penalizing the rejected answer.

ORPO solves this by adding a relatively small preference penalty to the standard SFT loss.
