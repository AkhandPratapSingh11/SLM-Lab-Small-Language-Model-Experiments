What is ORPO?
06 August 2026   13:15




Monolithic Preference Optimization technique without Reference Model

ORPO is a single-stage preference optimization method

monolithic preference optimization or Single Stage preference optimization method
meaning is instruction learning and preference learning are integrated into one objective and in one training
process.

ORPO combines Supervised Fine-Tuning & Preference Alignment into a single training stage.

Pretrained Model
     ↓
SFT Training + Preference Training - ORPO
     ↓
Instruction-Following and Preference-Aligned Model

ORPO directly trains Pretrained Model using chosen and rejected responses.

preference data for ORPO:
Prompt | Chosen Response | Rejected Response

It teaches the same model to:
1. Learn the chosen response through normal SFT loss.
2. Prefer the chosen response over the rejected response through an Odds Ratio loss.

ORPO does not require:
• Separate Reward Model
• Value/Critic Model
• PPO or reinforcement learning
• Frozen Reference Model

Why Were Preference Optimization Algorithms Like DPO and ORPO Introduced, and What Limitations
of Standard SFT Do They Address?

In normal SFT, the model is trained only on the correct or chosen response.
Example:

Prompt:
How should I treat a mild fever?
Target Response:
Rest, stay hydrated, monitor your temperature,
and contact a doctor if symptoms become severe.
The SFT objective increases the probability of the target response.

However, SFT does not explicitly tell the model:
This response is preferred.
This other response is rejected.
The ORPO researchers observed that while SFT increases the probability of chosen responses, it may also
increase the probability of rejected responses because both responses can contain similar language,
tokens, topics and writing patterns.
SFT has no direct mechanism for penalizing the rejected answer.
ORPO solves this by adding a relatively small preference penalty to the standard SFT loss.

What Does “Reference-Model-Free” Mean?
In DPO, two models are used:
Policy Model → Trainable
Reference Model → Frozen

The Reference Model acts as a baseline and limits how far the Policy Model moves from its original behavior.

A Reward Model does not directly provide a score; instead, the reward signal is automatically derived by comparing the
probabilities of the Policy Model and the Reference Model.

Chosen implicit reward high
Rejected implicit reward low
    ↓
Loss low

Rejected implicit reward high
Chosen implicit reward low
    ↓
Loss high
    ↓
Model is corrected


ORPO uses only one trainable Policy Model. It does not require an additional frozen Reference Model during preference
training.

Therefore, ORPO requires less memory and computation than DPO.

ORPO does not use a Reward Model or a Reference Model. The preference signal is derived by comparing the odds of
chosen and rejected responses from the same Policy Model, while SFT loss trains the chosen response.

Chosen odds high
Rejected odds low
    ↓
OR loss low

Rejected odds high
Chosen odds low
    ↓
OR loss high
    ↓
Model is corrected

Benefit and Limitations of ORPO
06 August 2026   13:17


Top 5 Major Benefits of ORPO
1. No Separate Reward Model
2. No PPO or Reinforcement Learning Loop
3. No Frozen Reference Model
4. Single Training Stage
5. Lower Memory Requirement Than DPO


Top 5 Important Limitations of ORPO
1. Not Validated Beyond 7B in the Original Paper
2. Limited Comparison with Other Preference-Optimization Methods
3. Limited Domain and Dataset Generalization Evidence
4. Sensitive to Noisy or Low-Quality Preference Data
5. SimPO: Simple Preference Optimization with a Reference-Free Reward
   Not Consistently Better Than Newer Methods such as SimPO
     https://arxiv.org/html/2405.14734v1
